## Phase screens method
Analytical approaches to wave propagation in random media rely on highly restrictive assumptions^[https://sci-hub.se/10.1364/JOSAA.32.001357], -- such as the Markov approximation, Rytov perturbation theory, etc. -- which limit their applicability to weak and sometimes moderate turbulence. 
Moreover, ensemble averaging inherent to these analytical treatments provides only statistical quantities of the optical field, such as mean intensity, correlation functions or scintillation index, leaving us without access to individual realizations of beam spots and their spatial dynamics.

The phase screen method is one of the standard numerical techniques for modeling wave propagation through a randomly inhomogeneous medium such as atmospheric turbulence.
The main idea is to represent the effect of turbulence along the propagation path as a sequence of thin phase-modulating layers ("phase screens") separated by free-space propagation intervals.
Each screen imposes a random phase distortion corresponding to the integrated refractive index fluctuations over a short segment of the path, while free-space propagation between screens accounts for diffraction and interference effects.

### Split step operator
To formalize this idea we rewrite the paraxial wave equation [@sec:parwave] in the form of the first-order differential equation with an operator on the right-hand side
$$\frac{\partial u(\mathbf{r};z)}{\partial z}=\bigg[\,\underbrace{\frac{i}{2k}\Delta_\mathbf{r}}_{\hat H_d} +\underbrace{ik\,\delta n(\mathbf{r},z)\vphantom{\frac{1}{1}}}_{\hat{H}_t(z)}\,\bigg] u(\mathbf{r};z) \equiv \hat H(z)\, u(\mathbf{r};z)$$
where $H_d$ corresponds for contribution from diffraction, $H_t(z)$ corresponds for contribution from turbulence $H(z)=H_d + H_t(z)$. In general, as the derivative and the function, they don't commute: $H_d H_t\neq H_t H_d$.
The solution to this type of equations can be formally written in the operator form
$$u(\mathbf{r};z) = \tau\,\mathrm{exp}\left[\int_{0}^{z}\hat H(z')dz'\right] u(\mathbf{r}; 0)$$
where  $\tau$ denotes the space-ordering operator, introduced by analogy with the Dyson series, as
$$
\begin{align}
\tau \exp\left[\int_{0}^{z} \hat{H}(z')dz'\right] = 1 + \int_{0}^{z} \hat{H}(z') dz' + \int_0^z \hat{H}(z') dz'\int_0^{z'} \hat{H}(z'') dz'' + \\
+ \int_0^z \hat{H}(z') dz'\int_0^{z'} \hat{H}(z'') dz''\int_0^{z''} \hat{H}(z''') dz''' + \dots
\end{align}
$$
where $z > z' > z'' > z''' > \dots$. 
We expand it to the second order in $z$ 
$$
\begin{align}
\tau \exp\left[\int_{0}^{z} {H}(z')dz'\right] = 
\textcolor{mygreen}{1 + {H}_d z + \int_0^z {H}_t(z') dz'} + \\ + 
\textcolor{mygreen}{\frac{1}{2} H_d^2 z^2}+ 
\textcolor{myred}{H_d\int_0^zdz'\int_0^{z'}H_t(z'')dz''} + \\ +
\textcolor{myred}{\int_0^z H_t(z') H_d z' dz'} + \textcolor{mygreen}{\int_0^z H_t(z') dz' \int_0^{z'} H_t(z'') dz''} + O(z^3)
\end{align}
$$
Let's consider the following ansatz (with regular exponentials) and expand it to the second order 
$$
\definecolor{mygreen}{RGB}{34,139,34}
\definecolor{myred}{RGB}{178,34,34}
\begin{align}
\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}H_dz\right) \exp\!\left(\int_0^z H_t(z')dz'\right) \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}H_dz\right) = \\ =
\textcolor{mygreen}{1 + H_dz + \int_0^z H_t(z')dz' + \frac{1}{2}H_d^2z^2} + \textcolor{myred}{\frac{1}{2}\bigg[H_d\,z \int_0^z H_t(z')dz' +} \\
+\textcolor{myred}{z\int_0^z H_t(z') H_d dz'} + \textcolor{mygreen}{2\int_0^z H_t(z') dz' \int_0^{z'} H_t(z'') dz''} \bigg] + O(z^3)
\end{align}
$$
comparing it with the previous expression we can write 
$$
\begin{align}
\tau \exp\left[\int_{0}^{z} \hat{H}(z')dz'\right] = \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}H_dz\right) \exp\!\left(\int_0^z H_t(z')dz'\right) \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}H_dz\right) + \\ +
\textcolor{myred}{H_d\int_0^zdz'\int_0^{z'}H_t(z'')dz'' + \int_0^z H_t(z') H_d z' dz' - \frac{1}{2}H_d\,z \int_0^z H_t(z')dz' - z\int_0^z H_t(z') H_d dz'} + O(z^3)
\end{align}
$$
Then, if the turbulence term can be represented as a Taylor series in $z$ as $H_t(z) = H_t(0) + H'_t(0) z + H''_t(0)z^2 + \dots$  then all the integral terms are vanished out (for the $0$-th order of $H_t$ series) or become of order $O(z^3)$. 
Finally we can write
$$
\begin{align}
u(\mathbf{r};z) \approx \underbrace{\exp\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\frac{i}{2k}\Delta_\mathbf{r}\right)}_{\text{vacuum prop. on } \\ z/2} \exp\!\left(i\underbrace{k\!\int_0^z \delta n(\mathbf{r},z')dz'}_{\phi(\mathbf{r}) \text{ - phase screen}}\right) \underbrace{\exp\!\left(\frac{z}{2}\frac{i}{2k}\Delta_\mathbf{r}\right)}_{\text{vacuum prop. on } \\ z/2} u(\mathbf{r}; 0)
\end{align}
$$
That means that for light beam propagation in turbulent atmosphere the diffraction impact and turbulence impact can be considered as decoupled with the 2-d order accuracy by $z$. 
This opens the possibility for efficient numerical simulation of light propagation by propagating the initial field $u(\mathbf{r};0)$ like in vacuum on the half of the total distance, applying the _integrated/cumulative_ phase perturbation induced by index of refraction perturbation -- $\phi(\mathbf{r})=k\!\int_0^z \delta n(\mathbf{r},z')dz'$ - phase screen -- and propagating this perturbed field again in vacuum on distance $z/2$.
